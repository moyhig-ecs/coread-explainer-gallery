Co-reading explainer gallery

Audited, interactive figures of what a language model writes when asked to reason about a geometric configuration — built only from the frozen outputs of pre-registered measurements, with the cards included verbatim. Read as one line of three stations.

Co-reading is a prototype for people reading together what an AI has generated — in course material as well as in research with AI. The most important acceptance condition is can the next step be seen? Two makers are there to find the variation: the received version is made by weighing their difference, not by picking a winner.

  1. moy-2 — from algebraic analysis to elementary geometry
    A reflection formula is offered to OLMo-3 32B base as a tool. Does the model compute with it, and what does it then write in its proof field? Cards olm113 → olm127 and the frontier controls.

  2. moy-1 — elementary geometry
    OLMo-3 32B answers one two-circle problem (the four mixed tangent intersections lie on the circle with diameter O1O2) asked open, and with one added sentence that blocks the easy answer “they are concyclic”. Then the 20 cards frozen after the first figure (olm76 → olm128), as one flow up to the next question.

  3. moy-5 — how to make a model draw the auxiliary line
    Frontier models answer one parabola problem whose apex is a mean proportional (Menaechmus’s symptoma). In most arms the answer field states only the floor and the apex stays in the reason field; it reaches the answer field when the question’s point set changes, when roles and the proportion form are given, or at effort max. Then 20 later cards read the road inside OLMo-3 32B. The board is drawn as a section of a cone: the parabola lies in the plane through the line FVD, perpendicular to the board.
